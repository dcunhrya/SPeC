Stanford Biomedical Perception Challenge

SPeC asks multimodal models to recognize, localize, and characterize visual features across biomedical images — not just to guess the downstream answer.

Organized by Stanford MARVL under Dr. Serena Yeung-Levy, built on the MMBU benchmark (arXiv:2606.06696).

Motivation

Biomedical MLLMs need two capabilities: perception (recognizing and interpreting visual features) and cognition (integrating those observations with prior knowledge to reason). Recent work has emphasized reasoning; emerging evidence indicates that visual perception remains the primary bottleneck for reliable downstream reasoning.

The scale of the data makes that bottleneck urgent. In healthcare alone, approximately 700 million medical imaging examinations are performed worldwide each year, alongside an estimated 6.4 billion glass slides generated annually in anatomic pathology. Electron microscopy can produce petabytes of data; high-throughput and spatial imaging can generate billions of images. Much of this latent knowledge is generated and stored, yet remains largely unexplored.

Models can often produce a correct downstream answer without understanding the image — shortcut learning from language priors or spurious correlations. SPeC exists to measure and advance genuine visual perception, not answer-only accuracy.

What SPeC challenges you to do

SPeC challenges multimodal models to improve perceptual capability by recognizing, localizing, and characterizing visual features across diverse biomedical images spanning imaging modalities, biological scales, anatomical regions, and clinical contexts.
